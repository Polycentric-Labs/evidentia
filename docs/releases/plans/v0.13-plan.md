# v0.13 plan: the shared spine and the correctness debt

**Status: RATIFIED 2026-08-28 at cycle open** (drafted 2026-08-22 at v0.12.0
release prep). All twelve open questions are answered in the ratified-answers
section at the foot of the file. This plan opens the first cycle of
a changed trajectory (below), so it supersedes the sequencing assumption in
[`v1.0-prep-plan.md`](v1.0-prep-plan.md) without superseding that plan's
content: every item in it still has to happen, later in the line rather than
next.

Scope philosophy: **EXPAND, on the reuse ordering.** v0.13 builds what three or
more sectors share, fixes what is already wrong, and sequences nothing ahead of
the licence answer or partnership it depends on. Single-sector work waits.

---

## The trajectory change

The maintainer's decision, recorded here because it re-shapes every plan
downstream of it:

**v1.0 is now the hardened, feature-complete, full-GUI-parity release, not the
next release.** The expansion happens first, across v0.13 and the 0.x cycles
after it. v1.0.0 tags when the feature surface is built out and hardened, and
the API-stability freeze then freezes something whole.

What that changes, concretely:

| Before (v0.12 plan, v1.0-prep plan) | Now |
|---|---|
| v1.0 is the next cycle: CONVERGE, not expand | v1.0 is the last cycle of the 0.x line, on the same CONVERGE terms |
| Feature expansion waits for v1.1 | Feature expansion happens in v0.13 to v0.16, before the freeze |
| The freeze covers the surface as it stood at v0.12.0 | The freeze covers the built-out surface, which is the point of moving it |
| Reviewer outreach starts at the next cycle open | Reviewer outreach starts when the feature surface is done, against a candidate worth reviewing |

The argument for it is the one the feature-opportunity research verified rather
than asserted: **no locked clock lands before v1.0.** CRA has no schema to
target, CR26 mandatory adoption is 2027-01-01, EU AI Act Chapter III is
2027-12-02, and CMMC Phase 2 is suspended. Nothing external forces v1.0 to be
next. The one dated item inside the window is the FedRAMP `FRC-CSO-JSN` rule
(optional 2026-07-04, maintain 2027-01-01), which is an open question below
rather than a settled placement.

### What this does to v1.0-prep

[`v1.0-prep-plan.md`](v1.0-prep-plan.md) stays valid and stays CONVERGE. Its
sequencing (freeze execution, the `Finding` rename, the frozen candidate, the

## The ordering rule

Both research passes converged on the same organising principle, and it is the
rule this plan follows:

> Build what three or more sectors reuse before what one sector needs, and never
> sequence a build ahead of the permission or partnership it depends on.

The corollary the sector work made explicit: the shared control spine (MFA,
logging, encryption, inventory, scan cadence, incident clock, retention,
training) is a canonical internal control set that every sector catalog maps
into. Building it once and mapping many vocabularies onto it is worth more than
any single crosswalk, which is why this cycle leads with collectors and a
primitive rather than with catalogs.

### Group A: the cross-cutting primitive

**V13-01. Temporal continuity and cadence assertion layer** over the append-only
store. One capability satisfies HITECH section 13412's twelve-month look-back,
GLBA 16 CFR 314.4(d)'s semiannual and annual cadences, PCI DSS 11.6.1's weekly
gap-free series, NERC CIP-007 R2's thirty-five-calendar-day evaluation, and IRS
Pub 1345's weekly ASV scan.

This is the single highest-value item in the cycle and the two research passes
reached it independently: the sector work called it "the thing the engine can do
that spreadsheets structurally cannot" and named it the differentiator every one
of the seventeen sector files arrived at on its own; the feature-opportunity work
ranked an evidence-freshness and cadence policy engine fourth on its slate with
the note that it is more foundational than another connector.

Two constraints carried forward from the research:

- The output must not imply that freshness equals compliance. A gap-free dated
  series is evidence of cadence, and nothing more.
- Recurring obligation coverage splits cleanly: retention and immutability
  *configuration* is machine-derivable, but *completeness of the record set* is a
  scoping judgement and must not be asserted.

V13-05 is this layer's first real consumer, so build them together.

**V13-22. Lift the `requires-python` cap to `<3.15` and support Python 3.14.**
Closes issue #212. The removal trigger the root `pyproject.toml` documents is now
met, and the `uv lock` trial it demands has been run.

The cap exists for two reasons, and only one of them is honesty. `litellm` (a hard
dep of `evidentia-ai`) used to declare `requires_python "<3.14,>=3.10"`, so the
stack genuinely could not run on 3.14. But the cap is also **load-bearing for
resolution**: without it, uv's universal resolver (which Dependabot runs) explores a
`python_full_version>=3.15` fork where `py-ocsf-models` collided with the newest
`cryptography`, producing "requirements unsatisfiable". When that happens **every
Dependabot run errors and no dependency PR can rebase**. That is the mechanism
behind the recurring dependency-PR breakage, and it is why this item is worth more
than the version number suggests.

Evidence gathered at drafting, not assumed:

| Package | Declares now | Allows 3.14 |
|---|---|---|
| `litellm` 1.98.0 (inside our `>=1.96.2,<2.0` pin) | `<3.15,>=3.10` | yes |
| `py-ocsf-models` 0.10.0 (the documented collision source) | `<3.15,>=3.10` | yes |
| `redisvl` 0.26.0 (the transitive the comment warned might still gate) | `<3.15,>=3.10` | yes |

A real trial in a throwaway checkout, with the cap lifted across the root and all
eight members: `uv lock` resolved **257 packages, exit 0**, and
`uv sync --python 3.14 --all-packages` resolved the full set cleanly. No
unsatisfiable fork.

**Lift to `<3.15`, not to unbounded.** Matching litellm's actual declaration keeps
the `>=3.15` fork pruned, so the resolution property that makes the cap load-bearing
is preserved rather than traded away for a bare version bump.

Remaining work, and the reason this is a v0.13 item rather than a five-minute edit:
resolution is not runtime. The suite has not been run on 3.14. Scope is (1) lift the

## The GUI-parity cost note

**Read this before believing any estimate above.**

Live state, from [`parity-coverage.md`](../../parity-coverage.md), which
`scripts/check_parity.py` generates from
[`cli-gui-parity.yaml`](../../cli-gui-parity.yaml):

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| GUI coverage | **100.0 percent** |
| `full` (CLI verb + API op + wired GUI route) | 104 |
| `api-only` | 0 |
| `cli-only` | 0 (ratchet floor: 0) |
| `exempt` (CLI-only by design) | 13 |
| Total live CLI leaves | 117 |

The gate asserts five invariants against live state: completeness (a new verb
cannot land unclassified), api-existence, gui-existence (including a check that
the route is wired through the runtime client rather than only compiling against
the generated types), the debt ratchet, and inverse-completeness (a new endpoint
cannot land unclassified either).

Two things follow, and the second is the one that costs money:

1. **The ratchet floor is 0, so no new CLI leaf can land as `cli-only`.** That is
   mechanically enforced. Every new leaf must be `full`, `api-only`, or `exempt`
   with a stated reason.
2. **The published 100 percent is not itself gated.** A leaf can land `api-only`
   and the gate still passes, while the coverage number quietly falls below 100.
   Holding the number is a discipline this plan has to budget for, not something
   the script will do on the plan's behalf.

**So every new CLI leaf in v0.13 needs a console surface, and that roughly
doubles per-feature cost.** A collector is not a collector plus a flag; it is a
CLI leaf, a REST operation, an OpenAPI entry, regenerated frontend types (the
drift gate enforces this in CI), a React route wired through the runtime client,
and the frontend typecheck, build and test pass. **Treat every S in the scope
table as an M and every M as an L.** That is the honest planning number.

How many new leaves v0.13 adds depends on two shape decisions that are open
questions below: whether the cadence layer (V13-01) is a new CLI group or an
extension of existing verbs, and whether the seven zero-credential resolvers
(V13-07) are seven leaves or one leaf with a selector. The plausible range is
roughly eight to sixteen new leaves against a current 117, with each coverable
one owing a console screen in the same cycle.

### v1.1 RESEARCH CANDIDATE: a standalone Rust verifier binary

**Not scheduled. Not before v1.0. Recorded here so it is not re-litigated from
scratch, and flagged as needing a research pass before anyone commits to it.**

Origin: a 2026-08-23 question about whether Evidentia should be rebuilt in Rust.
The answer to *that* question is **no**, and the reasoning should be kept so it
does not get re-asked every few cycles:

- The moat is **domain content and provenance** (97 catalogs, 16 crosswalks, the
  CR26 first-mover position, signed OSCAL emit), none of which improves in Rust.
- There is **no performance problem**. `docs/benchmarks.md` puts `catalog list` at
  roughly 150 ms including interpreter startup, and the real workloads are
  I/O-bound (cloud and SaaS APIs) or human-paced (an auditor reading a gap report).
- The **Python dependency stack has no mature Rust equivalent**: `litellm`,
  `compliance-trestle` (OSCAL), `instructor`, the MCP Python SDK,
  `sigstore-python`. A rewrite means hand-rolling OSCAL tooling in a project whose
  pitch is that it does not hand-roll compliance primitives.
- **Opportunity cost is the decisive argument.** A faithful rewrite of 8 packages
  and 5,000+ tests is 12 to 24 months with no new catalogs, no collectors and no
  v1.0, against a 541-row build register and a reuse-first cycle ordering that a
  rewrite discards entirely.
- It would also **re-run the bug class v0.12.0 was spent eliminating**: every
  invariant and gate re-derived by hand, when this project has repeatedly shipped
  false claims doing exactly that carefully.

What survives that analysis is **not a rewrite**. It is a second, small artifact:
a statically-linked `evidentia-verify` binary that verifies a signed evidence
bundle or OSCAL artifact offline, with no runtime to install.

Why this one is worth researching rather than dismissing:

- **Air-gapped and regulated buyers.** Installing a Python runtime is a
  procurement event in the environments this project targets. A single ~5 MB
  binary handed to an auditor is a different product experience, not a faster one.
- **Independence is the feature.** A verifier that shares no code with the producer
  is a *stronger* assurance claim, not duplicated work. Cross-implementation
  agreement is itself evidence, which is the argument the project already makes
  about machine-checkable output.
- It matches the ratified citation-currency framing verbatim: *a portable,
  independently re-verifiable signed attestation*. Portable and independent are
  exactly what a static binary buys.
- Scope is naturally bounded and honest: verify signatures, check hashes, validate
  schema. No LLM, no collectors, no OSCAL authoring.

**Open questions a research pass must answer before this becomes scope** (none is
settled, and the case above is argued from in-repo evidence plus general knowledge,
not from primary sources):

1. Is `sigstore-rs` mature enough to verify what `release.yml` actually produces
   (cosign keyless OIDC, SLSA provenance v1, PEP 740 attestations)? Verify against
   its own release history and open issues, not its README.
2. Is there any usable OSCAL handling in Rust, or does schema validation alone
   suffice for the verifier's bounded scope?
3. What do comparable assurance tools actually do here? Find real precedents of
   compliance or supply-chain tooling that shipped a second-language verifier, and
   any that tried and reverted.
4. Does a second toolchain break the release pipeline's guarantees? The current
   story is 8 PyPI packages plus one signed container; adding cross-compiled
   binaries adds a build matrix, a distribution channel and a new attestation
   surface, each of which is a place for the provenance chain to develop a seam.
5. Who is the verifier actually for, and would they use it? This should be
   answered from the sector research and the collaboration register, not assumed.

**Recommendation of record: keep the core in Python; revisit the verifier after
v1.0 ships, gated on the research pass above.** If Evidentia is ever wanted as a
fast pre-commit gate inside other people's CI, the calculus changes, but that is a
different product and belongs with the spin-off analysis, not here.

## Standing constraints carried from the research

Four constraints that apply to every item above and to every cycle after it.

1. **No dollar figure ships without a primary-source re-check.** Nearly every
   civil-penalty maximum in the research is marked UNVERIFIED, and OMB
   Memorandum M-26-11 (2026-04-17) cancelled the 2026 inflation adjustment
   because BLS did not publish October 2025 CPI-U, so 2025 levels persist.
2. **Signature is a differentiator, not a compliance necessity.** The full
   consolidated CR26 ruleset was parsed and "digital signature" does not appear
   as a requirement. Claims must say what signing buys, not imply a rule requires
   it.
3. **First-mover claims survive only as "first among open-source tools."**
   Established commercial platforms (Drata, Vanta, Secureframe, Paramify,
   stackArmor) already hold FedRAMP 20x authorizations per a GSA marketplace
   export dated 2026-08-21. The OSS-scoped phrasing is the honest one.
4. **Two access facts that cost engineering time if missed.** eCFR
   302-redirects automated fetchers to an unblock page, so build against GovInfo
   bulk XML or the documented API with a User-Agent; and eCFR updates daily while
   the GovInfo annual CFR updates once per year per title, so the two can
   disagree by up to a year on any given title.
